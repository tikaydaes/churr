churr

Chat that looks like it was built by the people who run it. Flat neon on navy, hard edges with one corner cut off, loud narrow headings, and labels that sit a little crooked. Calm where you read, sharp everywhere else.

principles

what makes it ours, and not Slack or Discord

01

Cut, don't round

No rounded corners anywhere. Things that matter lose their top-right corner instead. One notch per object.

02

Flat and hard

Solid colour only. Depth is a hard offset block, never a blur. No gradients, no glass.

03

Fills, not frames

Areas are told apart by steps of navy, not by boxes and rules. Monospace is kept for times, counts, code and tags.

04

Stickers sit crooked

Badges, tags and the "new" marker are rotated a couple of degrees. Reading text never is.

05

Glitch is a treat

The split-colour glitch is for the wordmark, the start-up screen and empty states. Never on text you have to read.

colour

four neons, each with one job

  • Lavender is the brand and "yours": your mentions, your selection, primary structure lines.
  • Cyan is live and go: links, the main action, online, the current channel, the cursor.
  • Pink wants attention: new messages, alerts, destructive actions, do not disturb.
  • Yellow counts and points: unread badges, keyboard focus, highlights, away.
  • Navy is the room. Three steps of it (ground, panel, raised) do all the layering.
  • The light theme keeps the same roles on a pale lavender paper, with the neons deepened so they still read.

contrast and colour vision

measured on this page, in the theme you are viewing

  • Text is at least 4.5 to 1 against what it sits on. Large headings, icons and the edges of controls are at least 3 to 1.
  • Each neon has a partner colour for text placed on it. Nobody picks that by eye.
  • Yellow is a fill, never a text colour, on light backgrounds. Where yellow text is wanted the light theme uses a dark amber.
  • Colour never carries meaning alone. Presence is a shape (square, hollow square, diamond). Your own vote has a tick and thicker edge. "New" is a word. Unread is bold plus a count. A mention is a filled block with an @.
  • A high-distinction palette is a setting in the apps and web chat. It swaps pink for orange and lavender for blue, so the four roles differ along blue to yellow and in lightness, which red-green colour blindness leaves intact.
  • The apps also follow the system's "increase contrast" setting by using the stronger edge colour for every line.

type

two open-licensed families, both SIL OFL

  • Archivo does the talking: messages, names, headings. It is one variable font with a width axis, so headings go narrow and loud and body text stays normal, without a second family.
  • Martian Mono is used sparingly: timestamps, counts, code, small tags and states like connected. Never for headings, buttons, section names or lists.
  • Message text is 16px and never mono, never uppercase, never tilted.
  • Both ship inside the apps and the server. Nothing loads from a font host.

Considered and set aside: Space Grotesk (everywhere already), JetBrains Mono and IBM Plex Mono (fine, but anonymous), a pixel face for headings (fun for a week).

icons

24px grid · 2px stroke · square ends · no curves where a cut will do

  • Drawn on a 24px grid with a 2px line, square line ends and sharp joins.
  • Corners are cut at 45° instead of rounded, echoing the notch. Circles are allowed only where the thing is round (a clock, a face).
  • Outline only. A solid fill means "on": a pinned pin, a starred star.
  • Starting point: the open Tabler set (MIT licence) for coverage, redrawn to these rules as each icon is used, so the set stays ours.
  • A public channel is marked # and a private one with a small padlock. A sub-channel is indented under its parent and nothing more: no tree lines.

controls

try them: hover, tab, click

  • The main action is a cyan block with the notch. It turns pink on hover and nudges 1px, as if pressed.
  • Secondary actions are a quiet raised block with a line under it.
  • The message box shows a blinking block cursor when empty. That is the one terminal touch.
  • Keyboard focus is always a 3px yellow outline, offset from the control.
  • Presence is a small square light, not a dot: solid cyan online, hollow yellow away, pink diamond for do not disturb, grey for offline.
  • A sealed chat has no label. Its presence light is a shield instead of a square: solid when online, outline when away, solid with a bar for do not disturb, dotted outline when offline.
  • Movement is quick and stepped, about a tenth of a second. Nothing eases or bounces.

message general

rules of the house

the short version for whoever builds a screen

do
  • Use the four neons for their jobs and nothing else.
  • Cut one corner on things that matter.
  • Keep mono for times, counts, code and tags.
  • Give every floating thing a hard offset block.
  • Tilt badges and tags, a little.
  • Say what a thing does not do, in plain words.
don't
  • Round a corner, blur a shadow or fade a gradient.
  • Tilt, glitch or uppercase anything people have to read at length.
  • Use colour alone to carry meaning; pair it with a shape or a word.
  • Add a fifth accent colour. Semantic states reuse the four.
  • Box things in. No bordered panels, bracketed buttons, prompts or tree lines.
  • Animate with easing or bounce. Step it or leave it still.
  • Load a font, icon or script from someone else's server.
